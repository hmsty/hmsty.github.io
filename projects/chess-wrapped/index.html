How you actually play

Five patterns the raw data actually supports — some useful, some just reassuring.

The rhythm of ups and downs

A rating doesn't climb in a straight line. It dips below its running peak and claws back, over and over — 22 times in this history so far.

Typical dip: 74 points, recovered in ~85 games / 11 days. The current one (rightmost, still open) is the deepest so far at 305 — four times typical, not a different kind of thing, just a bigger one.

Fatigue within a sitting

Streaks feel like a curse. They're not.

A permutation test reshuffled these exact results 5,000 times. Long streaks in either direction happen about as often by pure chance as they actually did here — longest win streak 10 games, longest loss streak 9.

After a loss

The "tilt" hypothesis: a loss puts you off and you lose the next one too. Score after a loss is 49.6% — essentially the same as after a win (51.1%). A 1.5-point gap over 1,765 games is noise, not a signal.

What is different: you queue about 2 minutes slower after a loss (13.0 min gap vs 10.7 min after a win). You're giving yourself a small mental breath, not rage-queuing — which probably explains why the tilt effect is so small.

White vs Black

A consistent 6-point gap: 53.7% as White, 47.5% as Black. This has not meaningfully converged across 19 months of play — the first-move advantage is real and persistent.

Longer games, better results (mostly not causal)

Winning positions take longer to convert; losing ones get resigned or abandoned fast — so most of this climb is reversed causality, not "play longer to win more." One real piece: as Black, quick wins are a third rarer than as White (12.3% vs 16.5%), even though quick losses are about even — consistent with the general first-move advantage.
