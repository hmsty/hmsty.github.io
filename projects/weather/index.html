            Data source, definitions and comparison with Google

            Source. Daily values come from the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API, which serves the ECMWF ERA5 reanalysis, a weather model constrained by observations, on a regular grid rather than from a weather station. The point used is . The period is 1991 to 2020 for the normals, plus the current year through for the year-to-date views.

            Normal. Formally, the average of a measurement over a fixed 30-year period, here 1991 to 2020, the period national weather services use. Loosely, people use it to mean typical, but a single day can sit far from the normal, which the percentile bands show.

            Percentile band. Formally, the 10th percentile is the value that 10 percent of days fall at or below, and the 90th is the value that 90 percent fall at or below, so the band holds the middle 80 percent of days. Loosely, it is the usual range.

            Rainy day. Here, a day with at least 0.1 inch (2.54 mm) of precipitation. Looser usage counts any day with a trace of drizzle, which gives far more rainy days. The 0.1 inch rule is the one that matches Google's Portland day counts in this data.

            Snowfall. Modeled new snow depth per day, converted from centimeters to inches. It is a model estimate, less reliable than a gauge measurement.
